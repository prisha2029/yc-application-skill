# Writing rules that apply to every answer

Each rule lists its YC/partner source codes (see `sources.md`). "Founders" counts are from the 12 verified accepted applications and the 19 verified video and interview accounts in `accepted-founder-cases.md`.

## 1. The answer goes in the first sentence
Reviewers read about 100 applications a day [PG-APPLY], and a single partner reads roughly a thousand per batch [KH-PITCH]. Use the inverted pyramid: conclusion first, detail after [GT-3TIPS]. Basedash S20: keep it to one or two sentences, with the first one as the summary.

## 2. Plain words, no buzzwords
Be matter-of-fact; marketing language says nothing [PG-APPLY]. Avoid jargon, acronyms and words like "platform" [MS-PITCH]. Great investors don't use buzzwords [GT-3TIPS]. A vague answer full of jargon is a major weakness [DC-2023]. Founders say the same: plain language comes up in 5 of the 12 applications, and Stilt W16 says jargon sounds fake.

**Check:** would a smart friend outside your industry understand it without asking a question? This is Seibel's "email test" [MS-PITCH]. Basedash and Slite tested theirs on outsiders.

## 3. Cut every unnecessary word
Every unnecessary word weakens the necessary ones [PG-APPLY]. Lingo.dev F24 halved its application on reapplying and got in. Metorial F25 also tightened.

## 4. Specifics beat claims
Give one concrete fact rather than an adjective. Specific examples beat claims about dedication [PG-APPLY; JL-2015]. 11 of 12 accepted applications put specific numbers in the progress answer, even tiny ones: Basedash had $30/month, and Paystack went from 22 to 318 transactions.

## 5. Never inflate, never misstate
Misstated traction disqualifies you; presenting annual revenue as monthly is the classic example [DC-2023]. Honest mistakes are forgiven, deliberate misrepresentation is not [DC-2023]. Big claims, such as a famous customer, need proof [DC-2023]. Separate what exists from what you expect (Metorial F25).

**Claude rule:** never invent or round up a number, user, customer or credential. If a fact is missing, ask.

## 6. Name your obstacles before the reader does
Reviewers shouldn't be able to raise objections you haven't considered, and flaws you leave out look like oversights [PG-APPLY]. Self-awareness about the challenges is a plus; overconfidence is a red flag [DC-2018]. Accepted founders admitted weaknesses openly: Command AI said it had no defensibility, and Basedash said its traction was small.

## 7. Lead with the problem, not the technology
Lead with what, not why or how [KH-PITCH]. Don't start with the technology [KH-PITCH]. Metorial's rejected S25 answer opened with implementation details. Its accepted F25 answer opened with the product and the category.

## 8. Comparisons to known things work, carefully
Describing yourself as a variant of something known works well [PG-APPLY]. "X for Y" only works if X is a household success, Y actually wants X, and Y is a big market [KH-PITCH]. Basedash S20 adds that X must be famous. 8 of the 9 accepted one-liners were short and plain, often in "X for Y" form or a category name.

## 9. Fill in every field
Incomplete applications and bad grammar hurt [DC-2023]. If a question doesn't apply, answer it in one honest line rather than leaving it blank.

## 10. Don't overwork it
Basedash S20 warns against spending too long on the application. Hyperlink W20 applied an hour before the deadline. YC FAQ: a late application isn't treated as on time, so submit, then send updates for material changes. Metorial and Keywords AI both updated with new numbers after submitting.

## Red-flag scan (run on every draft)
Flag and fix:
- Buzzwords: platform, revolutionize, disrupt, leverage, synergy, AI-powered with no "to do what", seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing, ecosystem, end-to-end, world-class.
- An opening sentence that doesn't answer the question.
- Adjectives about the team ("passionate", "hard-working") with no evidence behind them. Passion is not an insight [MS-PITCH].
- "Our advantage is design / ease of use" [PG-APPLY].
- More than one business model [MS-PITCH].
- A top-down market size ("1% of a $50B market") with no bottom-up maths [MS-PITCH]. This is the most common self-critique from accepted founders: OpenPhone, Basedash and Metorial.
- "No competitors" [PG-18MISTAKES].
- Monthly and annual figures mixed, or any number without a time period.
- Future tense presented as fact.
- The startup itself given as a founder's "most impressive thing" [PG-APPLY].
