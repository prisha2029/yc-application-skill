# Question-by-question playbook

**Use the founder's live form as the source of truth.** YC changes its questions and limits, and the form sits behind a login. The questions below are the recurring ones. YC has published versions of them from 2007 (the Dropbox application [YC-DROPBOX]) and 2017 (WittyThumbs [YC-WITTY]), and accepted founders have quoted them in their published applications up to F25. If the founder pastes the current questions and character limits, use those exact wordings and limits.

Each entry gives: what the reviewer is checking → how to answer → what accepted founders did → pitfalls.

---

## Company

### Describe what your company does (one-liner, ~50 characters)
- **Checking:** can you say it clearly? Is it worth reading on?
- **How:** what you make, plus for whom. Plain words and no adjectives. Name a category, or use "X for Y" if X is famous and fits [PG-APPLY; KH-PITCH; MS-PITCH].
- **Accepted founders:**
  - Basedash S20: a collaborative database interface for teams, i.e. Airtable for your existing database.
  - OpenPhone S18: a phone system plus CRM for small businesses.
  - Metorial F25: switched from a code-level claim to a category name, the MCP integration platform for agentic AI.
- **Pitfalls:** "AI-powered platform that…", taglines, or needing a second sentence to make sense.
- **Claude:** draft 5 variants in different shapes (category, X-for-Y, customer+outcome, verb-first, problem-led). Run the email test on each. Recommend one.

### What is your company going to make? / What do you do?
- **Checking:** concrete understanding of the product.
- **How:**
  - Sentence 1 is the one-liner expanded: who uses it and what it does for them.
  - Then how it works from the user's side, in 2–3 sentences.
  - Then what exists today.
  - Lead with what, not why or how, and not the tech [KH-PITCH; DC-2023].
- **Accepted founders:** Metorial's rejected answer led with caching and cold starts. The accepted one led with what the product includes.
- **Pitfalls:** feature lists, vision statements, architecture.

### Where do you live / will the company be based?
Answer factually.

### Demo / product link
Give it if there is one. A separate demo video helped two reapplicants (Lingo.dev F24, webapp.io S20). Lingo.dev called skipping it the mistake behind its first rejection.

## Founders

### Each founder's background / most impressive thing built or achieved
- **Checking:** proof you can build and get things done. Determination is "what matters most" [PG-FOUNDERS].
- **How:** one specific accomplishment per founder, of real size, with a number or outcome. Not the startup itself, and no generic praise [PG-APPLY]. For technical founders, name what they built. Internships at strong tech companies count [DC-2023].
- **Accepted founders:**
  - Courier S19: rebuilt a legacy product that helped win deals before the company was acquired.
  - Slite W18: built a real-time Wikipedia racing game.
  - OpenPhone S18: shipped mobile products to 500K+ contractors, and grew a product to 200K+ users.
- **Claude:** ask "what's the hardest thing you've done that most people couldn't?" and push for the number.

### Tell us about a time you hacked a non-computer system to your advantage
- **Checking:** do you like to beat the system? Naughtiness: rule-breaking where the rules don't matter [PG-APPLY; PG-FOUNDERS]. A strong answer can rescue a weak application [PG-APPLY].
- **How:** a short true story covering the system, the trick, and the result. Clever, legal and not harmful. Keep it to 3–5 sentences.
- **Pitfalls:** anything unethical or illegal, a non-story ("I'm resourceful"), or a computer hack when they asked for non-computer.
- **Claude:** never invent the story. Offer prompts: bureaucracy navigated, a pricing or loyalty loophole, a school rule, a negotiation, a scheduling trick, a way of getting into a room you weren't invited to.

### How long have the founders known each other and how did you meet?
- **Checking:** whether the team will hold together. Founders who have known each other a long time [YC-FFC2016; MS-PITCH].
- **How:** years, where you met, what you've done together. Plain facts.
- **Accepted founders:** Metorial F25: 10+ years, from technical high school, and co-led engineering at a startup. OpenPhone S18: 2 years, met through a friend.

### Who writes code / technical work? Who is full-time?
- **Checking:** can you build it yourselves (YC expects that [YC-FAQ]), and are you committed?
- **How:** name each person and their role. Give full-time status now, or a specific date. Part-time with no date is a weak signal (Metorial and webapp.io fixed this before acceptance).

### Equity split / co-founder questions
Investors check whether the split is roughly equal [MS-PITCH]. Give the split, plus a one-line reason if it's unequal. See `situations.md`.

## Progress

### How far along are you? / Users, revenue, growth
- **Checking:** progress relative to time spent [MS-PITCH]. Product and customers count far more than fundraising or partnerships.
- **How:**
  - Lead with the strongest real metric.
  - Then the time context: how long you've worked on it, full-time or part-time.
  - Then the growth rate.
  - Give exact numbers with periods, labelled as monthly or weekly. Say whether users are paying.
  - If tiny, say so plainly. 5 of the 12 accepted applications had $0–$2K revenue, and Courier S19 had zero users.
- **Accepted founders:**
  - Basedash S20: $30/month after four months at $0.
  - Paystack W16: 12 pilot merchants and transactions growing month over month. Merchants left money with them before a payout system existed, which is trust evidence.
  - OpenPhone S18: 1,500 users and +200% month over month, with 32 paying within two weeks of billing going live.
- **Idea-stage:** user conversations (count, plus one quote-worthy finding), waitlist, LOIs, pre-payments. Relate S22: 8 prepaying companies before the product existed. Metlo-style $50 pre-payments also count.
- **Pitfalls:** mixing annual and monthly figures [DC-2023]; vanity metrics with no period; "we'll charge later".

### How long have you been working on this? Full-time?
Give the dates and who. Be honest.

### Revenue / how much do you make per month?
State it exactly and as monthly. Never round up.

### If you've applied before, what's changed?
- **Checking:** progress, and whether you answered the last objection.
- **How:** lead with the stated rejection reason and how you addressed it, then the new numbers. See `situations.md`, "Reapplying".

## Idea

### Why did you pick this idea? Domain expertise? How do you know people need it?
- **Checking:** founder–problem fit and real demand. Good ideas are organic: problems the founders have themselves [PG-IDEAS].
- **How:** a short personal story of the pain, then the evidence that others share it. Accepted founders: 10 of 12 ideas came from the founders' own problem.
  - Paystack W16 couldn't accept payments for his first startup.
  - Hyperlink W20 had a story about notifications revealing user drop-off.
  - Metorial F25 added outside evidence on reapplying: a waitlist and inbound interest.

### What's new about what you're making? What substitutes do people resort to?
Name the current workarounds concretely. Say what's 10x better [KH-2019]. "Better design / easier to use" isn't enough [PG-APPLY].

### What do you understand that others don't? (insight)
- **Checking:** a non-obvious truth from customers, competitors or personal experience. Passion is not an insight [MS-PITCH].
- **How:** one sentence stating the insight, then the evidence that you know it. Use "why now" (what changed recently in technology, regulation or behaviour) where it applies [JF-IDEAS].
- **Accepted founder:** Basedash S20: software is built by teams, not individuals.

### Who are your competitors? Who do you fear most?
- **Checking:** that you know the market. Competition is normal: you can only avoid it by avoiding good ideas [PG-18MISTAKES]. A crowded market signals demand [PG-IDEAS].
- **How:** group the competitors into categories, name the real ones, and say which you fear most and why. Then give your specific difference.
- **Accepted founders:** 8 of 12 named competitors and admitted which one was the biggest threat. OpenPhone S18 feared an unknown startup with the same vision. Paystack later regretted dismissing incumbents.
- **Pitfalls:** "no competitors"; dismissing big companies; an advantage that is just "better UX".

### How do or will you make money? How much could you make?
- **Checking:** that you've thought about it; a simple, plausible model [PG-18MISTAKES; MS-PITCH].
- **How:**
  - One model, usually the one that dominates your space, with a price.
  - Size the market bottom-up: number of customers times price per year [MS-PITCH].
  - Show the arithmetic in one line.
  - A small starting wedge is fine (Airbnb began with conference travel [GT-INT]).
- **Accepted founders:** OpenPhone S18: about $100/month across 33M small businesses, which they put at $40B. Metorial F25 calls its top-down sizing its weakest answer.

### Other ideas you considered
List 2–3 briefly. It shows range and flexibility.

## Equity / legal / curious
- **Incorporation, investors, IP, non-founder code:** answer fully and factually. See `situations.md`.
- **How did you hear about YC? / What convinced you to apply?:** one honest line.
- **Something surprising you've discovered:** a genuine, specific curiosity. Don't force a pitch into it.
