# Founder video: writing the talking points

Source codes refer to `sources.md`. "Accepted-founder" evidence is from founders whose companies are listed in YC's company directory.

## The rules YC itself sets [YC-VIDEO]
- About **1 minute**. Dalton Caldwell: one minute is "a perfect amount of time" [DC-2023].
- **Every founder appears.** If you can't be in one room, screen-record a video call. Splicing separate clips is acceptable [DC-2018].
- Content: introduce yourselves, say **what you're doing and why**. YC wants to see how the founders communicate.
- **Not a demo, not a promo.** Demos go in the separate demo section.
- **Don't read a script.** Speak from bullet points the way you'd explain it to a friend.
- Ignoring the video instructions reads as not being serious [DC-2023].

## What accepted founders say worked
- **Show the co-founder relationship.** It's the one place reviewers see how you interact (Basedash S20; Cherry W19).
- **Seriousness over polish.** Production quality doesn't matter (Basedash S20). Cherry W19 aimed to come across as determined and serious.
- **Pick one thing to highlight**: the strongest fact you have (Cherry W19).
- **Don't talk over each other.** Agree who says what in advance (Cherry W19; webapp.io S20 passed a mic in interviews for the same reason).
- **No gimmicks or stunts** (Cherry W19).
- **Multiple takes are normal.** Teams still felt awkward about the result and got in (Cherry W19, Hera S21). Don't chase perfection.
- **Add a separate demo video if you have a product.** Two teams that got in on reapplication cite adding one as a fix (Lingo.dev F24, webapp.io S20).

## What accepted founders' real videos show
Two accepted videos published by YC have readable transcripts: DoorDash S13 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rzlr2tNSl0U), [transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/ycombinator/doordash-s-application-video-for-yc-s13)) and Weave W14 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rpyvQJGcQGw), [transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/ycombinator/weave-s-application-video-for-yc-w14)). Both predate the current 1-minute guidance and ran 1:29 and 1:41, so copy their order, not their length.
- **Names in the first ~10 seconds, each with one credential** (2 of 2). For example, DoorDash founders gave their school and previous employer.
- **What we do by ~15 seconds**, in one plain framing line (2 of 2). Weave opened by calling itself a phone company.
- **Evidence of the problem in the middle.** DoorDash cited 100+ interviews with small businesses.
- **Each founder covers their own area** (2 of 2). The technical founder talks tech; the operator talks customers.
- **Hard traction numbers last** (2 of 2): paying customers, sales, revenue.
- **Neither said how the founders met** (0 of 2). That's optional on video if your written answers cover it.
- **Closing:** DoorDash ended on its numbers. Weave ended with a direct ask to YC, which today's guidance (no promo) argues against.

Nick Raushenbush (Shogun W18) recommends about 30 seconds on founders (name, role, one or two past companies, how you met), then about 30 seconds on the company (name, one-liner, what inspired it, one or two achievements) [[source](https://nickraushenbush.substack.com/p/y-combinator-application-breakdown)].

On jokes: on Hacker News, an accepted founder (Make School W12) said they made a silly video with accents, and a commenter who appeared to be from YC said humour wasn't why they got in and is generally a mistake [[source](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4715627)].

YC now lets companies publish their application video on their directory page. YC's video page links accepted examples: Kernel S25, Gumloop W24, Medplum S22, Whatnot W20, Teespring W13. Watch two or three of these before recording.

## The shape: 60 seconds, beat by beat
| Time | Beat | Who | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:12 | Intros | Each founder, a few seconds each | First name, role, and the one credential that makes you credible for this problem. No "Hi YC, thanks for watching". |
| 0:12–0:22 | What we do | CEO / lead | The one-liner, in plain words: what you make and for whom. |
| 0:22–0:38 | Why this, why us | Whoever lived the problem | The pain you saw firsthand plus the insight. Optional: years known / where you met, in one line. |
| 0:38–0:52 | Proof | Whoever owns the number | The single strongest fact: users, revenue, growth, pilots, or the number of user conversations if you're pre-launch. Exact and period-labelled. |
| 0:52–1:00 | Close | Lead | Where it stands and what's next, in one sentence. End on substance, not an ask or a slogan. |

Solo founder: same beats, all yours, still about 60 seconds. With 3–4 founders, each person still gets one beat; don't add time.

**Filming:**
- All founders in one frame if possible, or a screen-recorded call.
- Faces lit, quiet room, phone camera fine.
- No slides, screen share, music or B-roll.
- Unscripted bullets.
- Several takes is normal (Cherry W19, Hera S21). Stop at "clear and natural", not "perfect".

## How Claude should help
Never write a word-for-word script — YC says it hurts. Produce **talking-point cards**, one per founder, with a time budget:

```
FOUNDER VIDEO — target 55–65 seconds, all founders on screen

[Founder A — ~20s]
• Name + one-line background that makes them credible for THIS problem
• What we make (the one-liner, in plain words)

[Founder B — ~20s]
• Name + background / what they built
• Why us / how we know each other (years, where)

[Founder A or B — ~20s]
• Why this matters / the insight (one sentence)
• The single strongest proof point (users, revenue, growth, pilot)
• Close: what we're doing next
```

Then give a checklist:
- [ ] Under ~65 seconds on a timed read-through of the bullets
- [ ] Every founder speaks
- [ ] No slides, no screen share, no product demo
- [ ] Quiet room, faces visible, audio clear (phone is fine)
- [ ] Recorded from bullets, not read
- [ ] Demo, if any, recorded separately for the demo section

Adapt the split to team size. A solo founder covers all three blocks. With 3–4 founders, each person gets one short block, but the total still stays near one minute.
