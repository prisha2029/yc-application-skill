## Detailed but not verified on a16z's site
**Hayden Ko, OptimizerAI** (Seoul, generative game sound; says SR002): [blog](https://haydenko.inblog.ai/becoming-a-speedrunner).
- **Verification:** confirmed only by Korean press ([Wowtale](https://en.wowtale.net/2024/01/17/69729)). The company's speedrun page couldn't be loaded.
- **Timeline:** about 18 days from interview invite to offer.
- **Prep:**
  - studied the partners' podcasts
  - built a 6-slide deck of user insights and product changes
  - ran about 10 days of 15-minute mock interviews
  - prepared answers to personal questions
- **Questions he remembers:**
  - edge cases about how users would really use it
  - which phase of the customer's workflow needs the product
  - the team's strengths and weaknesses
  - what you'd do after succeeding
  - whether they'd expand beyond games
- **What he thinks got them in:** an all-engineer team, many customer conversations, and fast iteration.

Use this account as an illustration, not as proof.

## Patterns (out of 3 verified accounts, 4 including OptimizerAI)
- **The team and the vision carried companies with little product or traction:** 3 of 4 (Sitch, SUN, OptimizerAI). Note that Josh Lu ranked traction first for SR005 [SR-005REV]. The likely reading: traction helps, but a strong team can stand in for it at the earliest stage.
- **Candor and clarity over polish:** 2 of 4 (Smart Bricks, OptimizerAI).
- **Customer conversations or fast iteration as evidence:** 2 of 4.
- **A cold application worked:** 1 explicit (SUN).
- **The founder video:** 0 of 4 described it.
