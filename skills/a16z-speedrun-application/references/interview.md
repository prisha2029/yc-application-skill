# Selection process and interview

## Process
1. **Written application.** At least two humans read every application; no AI screening [SR-71Q].
2. **A possible request for a video and/or an interview** with an investing partner [SR-READ]. TechCrunch reports that about 10% of applicants reach a video call, but this isn't directly attributed to a16z, so treat it as unverified [SR-TC].
3. **First interview.** Partner posts say about 10 minutes, deciding whether to hold a longer 30–60 minute session [SR-WHAT]. The FAQ says 15 minutes, framed as a conversation that builds on your application [SR-FAQ]. Panels are reportedly 2–3 investors [SR-TC].
4. **Reference checks and follow-up calls, then an offer letter** [SR-READ].
5. **Decision timing:** usually 4–6 weeks [SR-FAQ]. One founder went from interview invite to offer in about 18 days (OptimizerAI; see the cases file). Closing targets about 2 weeks after acceptance [SR-FAQ].

## What they test (a16z's own list [SR-WHAT])
- The company in **one sentence**, and the vision at several levels of detail
- **Why you're uniquely positioned to win**, and why you'll beat competitors
- **Team chemistry and roles**, including hard decisions the cofounders made together
- For technical founders: have you thought about **hiring and fundraising**?
- Bring only the people who matter to the call.
- **Say the important things early.** Emily Bennett: catch her in the first two minutes [SR-ASK].

## What accepted founders report
- **Depth over polish.** Be candid about what isn't working, and explain the hard parts (Smart Bricks, Cohort 006).
- **Expect deep product questions:** product architecture, data strategy, long-term ambition (Smart Bricks). Also edge cases about how users really behave, which phase of the customer's workflow you fit, what happens after success, and whether you'll expand beyond your first market (OptimizerAI, not verified on a16z's site).
- **It's a conversation, not a pitch.** The invite email reportedly says so (OptimizerAI).
- **Team and dream can carry an early-stage company.** Sitch (Cohort 004) had a deck and a vision, no live product. It was accepted and deferred a cohort to rebuild.
- **Prep that worked (OptimizerAI):**
  - studied the partners' podcasts and talks
  - built a 6-slide deck of user insights and product iterations
  - ran 15-minute mock interviews for about 10 days
  - prepared personal questions such as "the hardest thing you've done"
  - prepared an update on what had changed since applying

Specific interview questions are **not published** by a16z.

## Mock-interview procedure for Claude
1. Read the fact sheet and the draft application.
2. Open with: "Tell me what you're building in one sentence."
3. Then rotate through:
   - why this team (and how you've worked together)
   - a hard decision you made together
   - your earned secret
   - your strongest competitor and why you win
   - traction or learning slope
   - 2 deep product questions on edge cases and user behaviour
   - hiring and fundraising plan
   - "what happens if this works — how big?"
4. One question at a time. Interrupt answers that don't get to the point in the first sentence.
5. After about 10 questions, give a scored list: the 3 answers to tighten, a rewritten one-sentence version of each, and any fact the founder should go and get.
6. Never invent facts for the founder.
